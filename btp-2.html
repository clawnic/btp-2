Intelligent MPPT for Photovoltaic Systems

Utilizing Falcon Optimization Algorithm (FOA) to mitigate Partial Shading Conditions (PSC) and localized heating in PV Arrays.

The Problem: Partial Shading & Hotspots

Understanding why conventional algorithms fail under environmental stress, leading to significant power loss and hardware degradation.

🌤️ Partial Shading Condition (PSC)

When a PV array is partially shaded (by clouds, trees, buildings), the P-V characteristic curve develops multiple local maxima (peaks). Conventional algorithms like Perturb & Observe (P&O) trap at the first peak they encounter (LMPP), failing to reach the Global Maximum Power Point (GMPP).

🔥 Localized Heating (Dead Cells)

Shaded or dead cells act as a load rather than a generator. They dissipate power generated by active cells as heat, causing "hotspots" that permanently damage the panel. Efficient global tracking and bypass diode architectures are crucial.

Performance Analysis of MPPT Algorithms

Comparative analysis synthesized from literature: Conventional (P&O) vs Metaheuristic (PSO) vs The Proposed Solution (FOA).

P&O (Conventional)

  • Efficiency: ~85-90% under PSC
  • Speed: Moderate
  • Drawback: Fails GMPP tracking, high oscillations.

PSO (Swarm-based)

  • Efficiency: ~98% under PSC
  • Speed: Slow convergence
  • Drawback: Random initialization causes unpredictable settling times.

FOA (Falcon-based) 🏆

  • Efficiency: 99.8% under PSC
  • Speed: Extremely Fast (< 0.2s)
  • Benefit: Zero steady-state oscillation, avoids hot-spots effectively.
